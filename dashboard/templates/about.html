About this dashboard

A quick guide if this is your first time here — what it shows, where the numbers come from, and how to use the filters.

What this is

This project started as a live alert — a small script that watches a handful of public sources for new whale sighting reports around West Seattle and sends an email or Discord message the moment one shows up, so there's time to grab a camera and get to a viewpoint. This dashboard is the second half of that project: instead of just reacting to the newest sighting, it keeps every sighting it's ever seen and lets you look for patterns across an entire season — which species and orca pods show up where, whether timing repeats, and whether conditions like tide or salmon runs line up with when whales actually appear.

It currently covers orca, humpback, and gray whale sightings (plus porpoise and dolphin, less commonly), reported anywhere in Washington's inland waters — not just West Seattle.

Where the data comes from

Sightings are pulled from Acartia, a public data cooperative for the Salish Sea that aggregates reports from observers, ferries, and partner organizations. Tide data comes from NOAA CO-OPS, and salmon abundance (used only for orca, see below) comes from Bonneville Dam's daily fish-passage counts. Full credits, including everyone Acartia itself credits as a founding contributor, are in the project README's Data Sources section — that's the canonical list if you want the details or need to cite something.

A quick honesty note: this dashboard currently holds a single season of data (spring through early fall 2026). Anything here that would need multiple years to really prove — like whether sightings repeat in the same months every year — says so directly on the chart rather than presenting one season's shape as an established pattern.

What each page shows

  • Home — a live count of sightings in the last 24 hours, broken out by species and nearest known location, plus at-a-glance KPIs: this season's total and week-over-week trend, the most active location this week, days since each species was last seen, and the most active orca pod this season. Species totals and region browsing live on Pivot tables and Map respectively.
  • Map — every sighting plotted where it happened, color-coded by species; orca sightings are further colored by pod (J/K/L/Bigg's-Transient) where that's known. Purely spatial — no charts or correlations live here on purpose, so it stays easy to read.
  • Analysis — four charts that go beyond raw counts (see below), with a date-range and species filter that applies to all of them at once.
  • Pivot tables — the same underlying counts as the charts, laid out as plain tables (pod × month, species × month, location × species) with row and column totals, plus all-time species totals, if you want the raw numbers.

What the Analysis charts mean

  • Sightings by tide state — whether sightings happen more often during a rising tide (flood), falling tide (ebb), or the short window around high/low tide (slack). Shown as a rate (sightings per hour), not a raw count — a raw count would make slack tide look artificially rare, since it's a much narrower time window by definition.
  • Tide height vs. sightings — the tide curve next to daily sighting counts (whatever species you've selected above), both scaled to a 0–100 range so they fit on one chart despite very different real units — useful for eyeballing whether tide height itself tracks sighting frequency, not just tide state (flood/ebb/slack).
  • Chinook salmon activity vs. orca sightings — daily Chinook salmon counts at Bonneville Dam next to daily orca sighting counts, both scaled to a 0–100 range so they fit on one chart. This only applies to orca — humpback and gray whales don't eat salmon, so there's nothing to compare there.
  • Sightings by season, by year — sightings grouped into winter/spring/summer/fall, with each year as its own bar. With only one season of data so far, this can't yet show a real year-over-year pattern, and the chart says so.

Using the filters

The Analysis page has a date range (From/To) and a species selector above the charts. Pick a range, Ctrl/Cmd-click to select one or more species, and click Apply — all four charts update together. The Chinook chart is the one exception: it's always orca-only no matter what you select there, since salmon abundance has no meaning for the other species. "Clear filters" resets back to the full dataset.
